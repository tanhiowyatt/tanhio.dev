## Main Problems of ICANN
![ICANN problems](./icann.webp)

The Internet is an integral part of our daily life. It provides access to, if not all, at least most of humanity's information, allowing it to be shared freely. Modern Internet platforms make it possible to quickly receive the latest news, scientific knowledge, and entertainment content. However, with the emergence of the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN), the hope for a free and independent Internet has come under serious threat.

ICANN, created in 1998 to manage the Domain Name System and IP addresses, initially seemed like a necessary compromise to ensure the stability and development of the Internet. Upon closer inspection, its activity raises concerns and negative assessments.

One of the key shortcomings of ICANN is its lack of accountability. As an organization with influence over critical Internet resources, it is important that it be accountable to the global Internet community. ICANN's complex governance structure, which includes stakeholder groups and advisory committees, has been criticized for lacking clear accountability mechanisms. The decisions made by ICANN have far-reaching consequences, leaving open the question of who ultimately is responsible for its actions.

The lack of transparency and openness of ICANN has always drawn criticism. The organization's activities, including policy development and decision-making, are insufficiently accessible to the public and all interested parties. This lack of transparency can lead to a situation where ICANN operates outside the control and influence of the Internet community, which has negative consequences for the Internet.

The influence of the United States government on ICANN is also a cause for concern. Despite steps towards increasing independence, the organization is still based in the United States. This geographical location raises questions about the American government's influence on its operations. ICANN's decisions may be subject to influence by the United States government, which could entail far-reaching consequences.

Control over top-level domains is another issue that raises serious concerns among critics of ICANN. ICANN's strive for control over domains such as .com, .org, and .net raises fears that the organization could use its powers in its own interests. This control could lead to abuse and violations of competition in the domain name sector, as well as create preconditions for monopolization, negatively affecting the structure and functioning of the Internet.

Issues of fair distribution of domain names also deserve attention. Distribution processes may turn out to be unfair, leading to advantages for large corporations at the expense of small businesses and individuals. This unequal access to domain names can seriously distort the competitive playing field on the Internet.

Internet freedom is one of the fundamental principles of the network. The concern is that ICANN's activities may threaten the freedom of information and access to it, especially in the context of potential regulation of domain names and IP addresses. This represents a threat to the Internet, where the free exchange of information and opinions is considered a key element.

## Case Studies

In 2016, ICANN renewed the contract with Verisign, the company responsible for managing the .com top-level domain. A significant aspect of this decision was the absence of open competitive bidding during the contract renewal. This action provoked a public reaction, including from Internet technology experts, and led to criticism regarding the lack of transparency in the decision-making process and compliance with relevant procedures.

The next important event was the entry into force of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in 2018. For ICANN, this led to difficulties in ensuring compliance with the new rules regarding the WHOIS database, which contains information about domain name owners. This event led to discussions and growing uncertainty among the Internet community and registrars on how to effectively comply with GDPR requirements while ensuring access to necessary information.

In addition, ICANN regularly faces administrative and legal disputes involving various stakeholders, such as registrars, registrants, and governments. These disputes can arise, for example, in the context of domain name suspension or cancellation, highlighting the need for compliance with legal and procedural standards in ICANN's activities.

The New gTLD expansion policy also attracted attention and sparked controversy. ICANN's decision to expand the range of available domains, such as .app, .blog, .guru, and others, triggered debates regarding which domains should be allowed and how the selection process for new top-level domain applicants should be conducted.

I would also like to discuss cybersecurity aspects. In the area of domain names and Internet infrastructure, the security and vulnerability situation is a significant concern. Security incidents and the discovery of vulnerabilities in this infrastructure have raised alarms, even among ICANN participants. These incidents have called into question the stability and security of the Internet as a whole, as well as ICANN's ability to respond quickly and effectively to such threats and ensure appropriate security measures.

## ICANN Structure and Reorganization

After examining the problems, let's dive deeper into the issues related to the internal composition of the organization and the decision-making mechanisms within it. While we previously touched upon aspects that influence the Internet, we will now turn our attention to those who shape these influences and which parties participate in this process.

ICANN is not just an administrative body managing the technical aspects of the network, but a center where strategic and policy decisions determining the future of the Internet are made. It has its own decision-making system involving various structures and groups representing the interests of different social and business sectors.

### Internal Composition of ICANN: Who Makes Decisions

- **ICANN Board of Directors (ICANN Board)**: This body consists of 16 members, including the President and CEO of ICANN. The Board of Directors makes strategic decisions, approves the budget, and oversees ICANN's activities. Board members are selected by representatives of different geographical regions of the world and public interests.

- **Stakeholder Groups**: ICANN includes stakeholder groups representing the interests of different sectors on the Internet. This includes the business community, technical experts, civil society, and even governments. These groups develop policies and recommendations for domain and IP address management.

- **Committees and Working Groups**: ICANN has many committees and working groups that handle different aspects of Internet resource management. These can be technical committees dealing with standards, or transparency and security committees.

- **Multistakeholder Model**: This is the fundamental principle of ICANN governance. It implies broad participation of all stakeholders in the decision-making process. The model is designed to take into account diverse interests and ensure the equality of all participants.

- **Decision-Making Processes**: Decisions in ICANN are made based on consensus, when the majority of participants agree with the proposed policy or change. The process includes public consultations, discussions, research, and impact assessment. Only after thorough analysis and discussion can a proposal be adopted.

### Internal Composition of ICANN: How Decisions Are Made

A domain goes through five "circles of hell" levels from a mere idea to implementation. Let's look closer at each of these "circles":

- **Determining the need for a new top-level domain**: The process begins with determining the need for a new TLD. This may be related to changes in the Internet, the needs of new business areas, or requests from specific communities.

- **Developing a proposal**: Stakeholders can develop a proposal to create a new TLD. This proposal must include a justification for the need for such a domain and a management plan for it.

- **Public discussion and consultation**: The proposal is subjected to public discussion and consultation. ICANN members and other interested parties can express their opinions and propose changes.

- **Decision-Making**: After public consultations and analysis of public opinion, ICANN decides to create or reject the new TLD. This decision must be made by consensus.

- **Registration and Management**: If a new TLD is approved, it is registered and becomes part of the domain system. Then its management and control are transferred to the institution or organization responsible for that domain.

### Reorganization

What do we see in the end? We see a huge structure designed to ensure transparency, but which is unable to actually achieve this goal and, it seems, continues to make decisions unilaterally from the top down. However, the question arises: can a reorganization help this organization chart a new course?

Some users propose the following plan of action to improve openness and transparency within the company:

- **Expanding the role and functions of the Oversight Board**: Strengthening the role of the oversight board, which will monitor transparency and ethical compliance in ICANN's activities, and inform the public about key decisions and events.

- **Strengthening Accountability Mechanisms**: Developing and implementing effective accountability and monitoring mechanisms that ensure regular publication of reports on ICANN's activities and financial status.

- **Mandatory Consultations and Feedback**: Introducing mandatory consultations with representatives of stakeholders before making key decisions, as well as establishing mechanisms for receiving public feedback and taking its opinion into account when developing policies.

- **Transparency of Financial Operations**: Open access to data on ICANN's expenses and income, including information on the salaries of executives and board members, to provide a clearer picture of the organization's expenses.

- **Independent Assessment and Audit**: Conducting regular independent audits and assessments of ICANN's activities, with results accessible to the public.

- **Expanding Public Participation**: Actively involving representatives of the public and observer organizations in solving important issues, for example, by expanding the number of seats on the ICANN Board of Directors.

- **Open Discussion Forums**: Conducting regular public forums and discussions where representatives of ICANN and the public can exchange views and ideas.

- **Strengthening Control over Ethical Standards**: Implementing strict rules and mechanisms to ensure ethical behavior of ICANN employees and board members.

- **Open Access to Judicial Decisions**: Guaranteeing open access to court decisions and lawsuits related to ICANN's activities.

## Ways to Solve the Problem

Let's dive into a plan that can truly solve ICANN's problems and stop trying to resuscitate the dead body that is this company:

- **Transition to a decentralized model** in the management of domain zones on the Internet. Switching to blockchain technology as a domain management tool will ensure more equal participation of stakeholders. Within this model, it is also planned to organize a migration process for current top-level domain (TLD) owners. In this case, TLD ownership is revised according to this approach.

- **Introducing new domain zones** based on user suggestions and their subsequent approval by voting, with the participation of validators, is a key aspect. This process ensures a more democratic and open management of the introduction of new domain zones. I should note that the decision to implement new zones is made after careful consideration and assessment by voting and validators.

Currently, there is a trend towards moving away from centralized management models in information technology. Many non-profit organizations using centralized management models face various problems, including collusion and political intrigue, which can impede neutral and fair management. In addition, such organizations may face challenges related to the legislative and regulatory restrictions of different countries, creating instability and risk for their activities.

In this context, the transition to more decentralized and democratic management models becomes an important factor. These models provide more opportunities for members of the Internet community to participate in the decision-making process and promote fairer and more transparent management. Such a transition helps avoid political intrigue and collusion, and also reduces the risk of negative impacts from legislative measures and restrictions on operations.

The trend of moving away from centralized management models towards more decentralized and democratic approaches in the management of domain names and Internet resources is a natural development that corresponds to the challenges of the modern information environment.
